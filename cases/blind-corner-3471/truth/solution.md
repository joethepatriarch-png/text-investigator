# SOLUTION — Blind Corner
> 🔒 FIREWALL — never shown, quoted, or summarised to the player.
> Read silently to adjudicate. The player learns this only by earning it.

## The truth, in one line
A grieving, ruined son smothered his dying mother's hospice nurse in a dark corner
of the West Town Mall lot — convinced she had stolen his inheritance — over a
fortune that never existed.

## What actually happened
Paula Hutchens, 46, a home-hospice nurse, spent the last five months of Eleanor
"Nell" Cate's life caring for her. A real bond formed. Grateful and lucid, Nell
had her attorney add a codicil leaving Paula her late husband Harold's restored
**1966 Ford Mustang** and **$10,000** — with a handwritten note asking Paula to
pass the car to Nell's little granddaughter, **Sadie**, when she was grown.

Nell's son **Brant Cate**, 51, was financially destroyed (failed HVAC business,
foreclosure, pending divorce, gambling debts at Harrah's Cherokee). He had staked
everything on inheriting his mother's grand Sequoyah Hills house — not knowing, or
refusing to believe, that it was reverse-mortgaged and the estate was effectively
**insolvent**. When Nell mentioned she'd "taken care of that sweet nurse," Brant's
mind curdled it into: *the nurse manipulated my dying mother and stole what's mine.*

After Nell died (~Jun 28), Brant began calling and texting Paula, escalating from
demands to threats. On **Sunday, July 19**, he asked to meet her late and quiet —
framing it as wanting to "clear the air about Mom" — at the dark overflow lot
behind the mall's vacant old Sears wing. Paula, kind and hoping to defuse him,
went. He got into her passenger seat, the talk turned to the will, and when she
tried to tell him the estate was worthless and the car was meant for *his own
daughter*, he smothered her against the headrest with a soft item. He took her
purse (and the phone in it) to fake a robbery and erase their text thread, left
her jewelry and car, and drove home to Farragut, ditching the purse on the way.

## Who did it, and why
**Brant Cate.** Motive: a delusion of stolen inheritance, powered by grief, shame,
and financial ruin. He needed to believe no one is kind without an angle — and he
killed to prove it, and was wrong about everything.

## The dramatic core
Brant murdered the one person who had shown his dying mother real tenderness — and
who was quietly safeguarding his own daughter's only inheritance — for money that
did not exist. Nell's note (in the estate file / among Paula's things):
> *"Paula — you were more a daughter to me these last months than my own blood
> knew how to be. Harold's car is yours. When Sadie is grown, see she gets it —
> she should have something of her grandfather's, and I trust you to know when."*

Paula had already told her daughter Maddie and her coworker Renae that she was
keeping the Mustang in storage to give to Sadie at sixteen. The house was gone to
the bank; the "fortune" was a mirage; the only real inheritance was a dead man's
car that the nurse was holding for the killer's child. Grief made Brant unable to
believe kindness was real, so he destroyed it — and orphaned his own victim's
fifteen-year-old daughter in the process.

## The evidence trail
1. **Scene / ME.** Staged-robbery inconsistencies: purse & phone gone, but jewelry
   worn and the ~$20k CR-V untaken; no ransack. Autopsy (Dr. Reyes, Knox County
   Regional Forensic Center) → **asphyxia by smothering**: conjunctival petechiae,
   no ligature, pressure bruising to the lip/gum, a contusion at the back of the
   head (pushed into the headrest). No gunshot/stab. Tox comes back **clean**
   (therapeutic sertraline only) — kills the overdose theory. No struggle-scatter;
   she admitted her attacker to the passenger seat → **she knew him.** TOD ~10pm–
   midnight Sun (July heat in the closed car widens the window).
2. **Victimology.** Vehicle registration → Paula A. Hutchens, Bearden; hospice
   nurse, Cumberland Home Care; divorced, one daughter (Maddie, 15, at her dad's
   that weekend — why no one missed her overnight).
3. **Phone/carrier records.** Personal phone missing, but carrier records show her
   last contact Sun evening: texts + a short call with a number resolving to
   **Brant Cate**, arranging to meet ~10pm "at the mall, back by the old Sears."
   Nothing to any drug contact.
4. **Employer (Cumberland Home Care).** Coworker **Renae Pruett**: Paula was
   beloved, a soft touch; recent patient **Eleanor Cate** died ~3 weeks ago; the
   son was "difficult," had been "hounding" Paula by phone; Paula meant to keep
   Nell's Mustang for the granddaughter. Med counts audit **clean**.
5. **Estate / will.** Attorney **Doug Sharp** confirms the **codicil** (Mustang +
   $10k to Paula) and Nell's note — and that the estate is **insolvent** (reverse
   mortgage on the Sequoyah Hills house). The imagined fortune doesn't exist.
6. **Brant's motive & means.** Public records: business dissolved, tax liens, home
   in foreclosure, divorce pending, gambling markers. He believed the house would
   save him.
7. **Placing Brant at the scene** (breaks his alibi):
   - **Cell tower** covering West Town Mall pings his phone ~9:45–11:15pm Sun —
     he claims he was home in Farragut all night.
   - **Kingston Pike cameras** (a gas station / dealership / bank / Ring doorbell)
     catch his distinctive **white Ford F-250** (ladder rack, Cate Mechanical
     ghost-lettering) heading to the mall ~10pm and away ~10:50pm.
   - **Forensics:** fibers on Paula's face/blouse match a jacket/moving blanket in
     his truck; his prints/DNA in her passenger area — and the Cates were
     estranged, so no innocent reason for it.
   - **Purse/phone** (if pursued) dumped in a dumpster/retention pond off Kingston
     Pike; phone last-locates near the mall.
8. **Collapse.** Tower + camera + forensics + codicil + financials break the alibi
   and the "hadn't spoken in weeks" lie. His estranged wife **Kelli** can confirm
   he was NOT home Sunday night and describe his fixation and rage about the will.

## Red herrings (medium)
- **R1 — Random mall robbery / carjacking.** Missing purse + the mall's crime
  reputation. Broken by: jewelry & car left, no other victims/pattern that night,
  smothering isn't a robbery MO, she admitted a known person to her car.
- **R2 — Drug diversion / overdose.** A hospice nurse with opioid access invites
  "she was dirty / she OD'd." Broken by: clean tox, clean med audit, smothering.
  Her brother **Denny Kirkland** (substance history, borrowed money) muddies it —
  but has an alibi (closing shift on camera at a Turkey Creek restaurant).
- **R3 — The ex-husband, Rick Hutchens.** Bitter divorce, custody friction —
  classic first suspect. Broken by: he was at their son's travel-ball tournament
  in Sevierville with other parents; settled split, no live financial motive. He's
  abrasive but innocent.

## Fail conditions (recoverable)
- **Tipping Brant early** (confronting him hard before evidence) → he lawyers up,
  cleans the truck, ditches the jacket/blanket. Recoverable via tower + camera +
  codicil + financials + Kelli, but harder.
- **Arrest on motive alone** (before tower/camera/forensics) → DA won't charge / a
  wrongful arrest has fallout. Recoverable — keep building.
- **Chasing the drug theory** (formal diversion audit) → costs days, dead-ends,
  and sours the agency's cooperation.
- **Never seeking phone/purse or tower data** → still solvable via camera +
  codicil + financials + Kelli, but slower.
