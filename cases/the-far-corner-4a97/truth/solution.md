# SOLUTION — The Far Corner
> 🔒 FIREWALL — never shown, quoted, or summarised to the player.
> Read silently to adjudicate. The player learns this only by earning it.

## The truth, in one line

Dana Kessler was beaten to death by her younger brother Cole — the brother she
raised — when she gave him one last chance to come clean about the money he'd
been stealing from their employer, and he chose himself over her.

## What actually happened

Dana Kessler, 41, was the bookkeeper and office manager at **Preston Heating &
Air**, a family HVAC company on Kingston Pike in Bearden. Her younger brother
**Cole Kessler**, 34, worked there as dispatcher / parts manager — a job Dana got
him. Doing the quarterly reconciliation, Dana found money bleeding out of the
company: payments to a vendor, "Summit Mechanical Supply," that matched no
deliveries, plus service calls paid in cash that never hit the books. Every
thread ran back to Cole's access.

She confronted him privately about two weeks before her death. Cole broke down
and told her the money was for his six-year-old daughter Sophie's medical
treatment — a leukemia scare, out-of-network specialists, bills insurance
wouldn't cover. **It was a lie.** Sophie is healthy. The money went to sports-
betting losses (Tennessee online books) and, past the app limits, a private
bookie Cole owed badly. Dana — who had raised Cole after their mother died, who
had bailed him out her whole life — believed the sick-child story completely. It
was the one story guaranteed to make her protect him instead of turning him in.
She agreed to cover the hole quietly and give him a chance to stop.

He didn't stop. Dana found a **fresh** fake invoice dated after their talk. Now
she was frightened: for old Hal Preston (who was like a father to her), that she'd
be blamed as the bookkeeper who missed it, and — though she wouldn't yet admit it
— beginning to doubt Cole. She told Cole they had to meet, away from work and away
from his family, to make a real plan: repay it in full on a schedule, or go to Hal
together before it surfaced. She chose the West Town Mall parking lot — public,
neutral, roughly halfway between their houses, private enough to talk at night.

They met about **10:15 PM**. Dana sat in her own car (a silver Honda CR-V), backed
into a far corner of the north lot toward Morrell Road; Cole got in the passenger
seat. She told him she couldn't keep covering it — he had to come clean to Hal or
she would, because it was coming out regardless and she would not go to prison for
his theft. To Cole, "come clean" meant prison, losing Brooke and Sophie, and —
worse — everyone learning the sick-child story was a lie and the money was
gambling. He panicked. He'd brought a heavy aluminum flashlight from his truck.
He hit her — several blows to the left side of the head. She died in the driver's
seat.

Then he **staged a robbery**: got out, smashed the driver's window from outside
with the same flashlight, reached in and took her purse (which also — a bonus he
realised only then — held the little notebook where she tracked the
discrepancies). He left her rings and the cash in the console, and knocked her
phone down between seat and console in his hurry. He wiped the passenger door,
walked to his truck parked a row over, and drove home. On the way he threw the
flashlight, the purse, and the notebook off the **Northshore Drive bridge** into
the water. Home by ~11:35 PM. He told Brooke he'd been "out with a buddy."

## Who did it, and why

**Cole Kessler**, the victim's brother. Motive: to keep his embezzlement — and the
humiliating truth behind it — from coming out, which would have cost him his
family, his freedom, and the sympathy he'd been trading on. He killed the one
person who had spent her life protecting him, in the middle of her trying to
protect him one more time.

## The dramatic core

Dana died believing she was shielding a desperate father saving his sick child.
She wasn't. There was no sick child. Cole had looked his sister — the woman who
raised him — in the eye and invented his own daughter's leukemia because he knew
it was the one lie she'd never see past, the one that would make her protect him
instead of turning him in. The tragedy the investigator uncovers isn't only that
her brother killed her. It's that her love was never repaid, never even real to
him — he weaponised it. The final gut-punch is documentary: in Dana's backup notes,
written the night before she died, *"whatever it takes, we keep Sophie's family
together — one last time."* She was still trying to save a child who was never
sick, from a man already deciding to kill her.

## The evidence trail

A competent investigator can reach the truth along this chain:

1. **Break the robbery framing.** The scene doesn't read as robbery/carjacking:
   car backed into a remote spot, engine off, keys in the console (she parked to
   wait, not carjacked); rings and console cash left; only the purse gone. ME:
   blunt-force from the **passenger side / above** — attacker seated inside, not
   blows reached through a smashed driver's window. Glass evidence: blood/tissue
   **under** the glass on the seat, no glass in the wounds → window broken **after**
   death. The known mall car-prowler has an alibi (in county lockup that night) and
   a nonviolent daytime MO. Conclusion: staged, and she knew her attacker enough to
   let them sit beside her.

2. **Why was she there at 10:30 PM?** Victimology + son Aidan: she left home late,
   stressed, to "deal with something about Uncle Cole and work," after a phone call.
   Carrier records (survive though the phone is gone): calls/texts between Dana and
   Cole that evening, incl. a ~9:50 PM call. She went to meet Cole.

3. **The money.** Hal Preston says Dana had seemed worried about "a mistake in the
   books." Dana's home computer / a thumb drive / emails-to-herself hold her
   reconciliation notes: "Summit Mechanical Supply" payments with no deliveries,
   off-book cash jobs. Access + the shell vendor's setup trace to **Cole**. (The
   notebook missing from the purse was her working copy; the backup at home is the
   trail.)

4. **Cole lies about where he was.** He'll claim home by 9, or "out with a buddy."
   The buddy denies it / can't cover 10–11 PM. Cell-tower data puts Cole's phone on
   a tower covering West Town Mall ~10:15–10:45 PM. An LPR or a Kingston Pike /
   Morrell Road business camera catches his truck near the mall around then.

5. **Forensics tie him to the car.** Touch DNA / a partial print on the passenger
   interior; wound morphology consistent with a heavy cylindrical object (flashlight);
   if the purse/weapon are recovered (a HIT), physical confirmation.

6. **The twist that makes it land.** When cornered, Cole's defence — and Brooke's
   honest belief — is the sick-child story. It collapses on a records check: Sophie
   has no oncology history; pediatric records are clean; Brooke, asked plainly, is
   baffled — Sophie's never been sick. Meanwhile Cole's finances show sportsbook
   losses, a second household of debt, and payments to a known bookie. Dana died
   for a lie.

## Red herrings (scaled to medium)

- **The robbery / carjacking framing.** The strongest early pull. Patrol likes it;
  dispatch mentions a rash of mall car break-ins. It is staged; the forensics break
  it (trail #1). Chasing the prowler burns time but resolves cleanly.
- **Greg Marsh, the ex-husband.** Bitter divorce, behind on child support, a prior
  assault/DUI, no clean alibi at first — a textbook suspect. But he was at a Bearden
  bar then with his girlfriend (bar camera + receipt + her statement), and crucially
  **had no way to know Dana would be at the mall** — the meeting was arranged
  privately with someone she trusted. Clears on checking.
- **Denny Preston, the owner's son.** Resents Dana's grip on "his" company, wanted
  to cut costs and push his father out; friction with Dana is real and witnesses
  report it. But he was out of town (a Nashville trade show, verifiable) and has no
  motive to kill — a personality-conflict distraction who may even push the robbery
  theory to keep scrutiny off the company's books.
- **Dana as the embezzler.** A cynical read: the bookkeeper skimming, killed by an
  accomplice. The access trail and her backup notes (documenting, not hiding)
  exonerate her — but a player can waste a turn or two here.

## Fail conditions (all recoverable)

- **Tip Cole early** (interview him as a suspect before you have the money trail or
  his location data) and he lawyers up, dumps his phone, and pressures Brooke to
  harden her false alibi. Recoverable via the paper/financial trail and cell/LPR
  records, which don't depend on his cooperation.
- **Arrest on the robbery theory** (charge the prowler) — DA won't hold it once
  forensics contradict; wasted days, but the real trail is intact.
- **Charge Cole on motive + presence alone** without the financial trail or the
  forensic tie — a defense attorney floats "grieving brother met his sister, left
  her alive; a prowler did it." Needs the books + location + (ideally) recovered
  weapon or DNA to stick. Keep building; it's reachable.
- The case only goes truly **cold** if the player abandons it or burns every thread
  (never gets the books, never checks Cole's location, fixates permanently on the
  prowler or the ex). Rare.
